About Us

A small room, a long bench, no hurry.

Maison Solenne began with a simple refusal: to make things quickly. Everything since has been an argument for the slower way.

The studio

One bench. One maker. Start to finish.

Most workshops divide the work — one person cuts, another stitches, a third inspects. We never adopted the habit. The person who begins your piece is the person who hands it to you, and their mark is struck inside where only you will find it.

It is not the fastest way to work. It is why a Solenne piece can always come home: whoever made it can repair it, resize it, or remake it, for as long as the atelier stands.

The materials

Gold with a past. Leather with a future.

Our gold is recycled and traceable. Our diamonds are old cuts, reclaimed from pieces that had stopped being worn — stones with a hundred years of light already in them.

Our leather is vegetable tanned, the slow method, the one that takes forty days in pits of oak bark. It darkens with wear instead of peeling. A strap should look better in year five than in week one, and ours do.

The craft, in short

Slow is a specification

A strap passes through six stages and two hundred and fourteen steps. A watch is regulated in five positions over nine days. A ring is soldered, filed, and polished until the join disappears — then the maker tries to find it under the loupe, and only when they cannot is it finished.
